# Use a qualified third-party mail protocol engine

Generic IMAP and SMTP connections will use a qualified third-party protocol engine behind the provider-neutral Mailbox Connection adapter rather than a product-owned wire-protocol implementation. The engine owns transport, authentication, protocol framing and parsing, MIME, and connection lifecycle; the product continues to own durable synchronization, local persistence, Stable Provider Message Identity, role mapping, capability policy, retries, privacy controls, and provider-action reconciliation.

## Candidate decision

[SwiftMail](https://github.com/Cocoanetics/SwiftMail) is the preferred candidate because it is actively maintained, supports the required Apple platforms, and provides async IMAP, SMTP, MIME, IDLE, XOAUTH2, UID-based operations, and explicit TLS modes. Release 1.8.0 is not accepted because its public move and copy operations discard the server's `COPYUID` source-to-destination mapping and its SMTP send API does not expose whether submission was rejected or has an ambiguous outcome. A future tagged release may qualify only if it exposes verified UID mappings, preserves that SMTP outcome distinction, and passes the acceptance gates below. The dependency will be pinned to the exact audited release; the product will not carry a patch, fork, or handwritten IMAP fallback.

[MailCore2](https://github.com/MailCore/mailcore2) and [Postal](https://github.com/snipsco/Postal) are rejected because their maintenance and Apple-toolchain posture do not meet the product's dependency requirements. Direct use of Apple's [swift-nio-imap](https://github.com/apple/swift-nio-imap) is also rejected because it is a low-level, pre-production building block whose use would make the product responsible for the high-level client implementation this decision intends to avoid.

## Acceptance gates

- A vertical spike must pass against both iCloud Mail and Fastmail before the engine becomes a production dependency. It must cover authentication, role discovery without folder-name inference, creation of a missing role mailbox, newest-50 metadata availability, UID/UIDVALIDITY reconciliation, complete-history backfill, selected body-part fetching, provider-backed full-text body search, read and unread changes, spam-state changes, IDLE recovery and cancellation, SMTP delivery, and verified Sent append. Deterministic and provider-backed search cases must prove a body-only phrase is found in the requested mailbox and that absent, mailbox-excluded, and header-only matches do not become false body matches. Deterministic and provider-backed mutation cases must prove each required provider action and permitted missing-role creation is exposed and reconciles to the expected server state.
- Authentication qualification must include deterministic password rejection plus XOAUTH2 success, challenge, and rejection cases, and a provider-backed XOAUTH2 case for each certified provider account that supports it. Every successful challenge path must return the same complete capability and mailbox snapshot as ordinary authentication, and successful IDLE recovery must retain XOAUTH2 credentials and challenge handling under both implicit TLS and STARTTLS.
- Secure-transport qualification must reject invalid certificates and hostnames under both implicit TLS and STARTTLS during initial setup and IDLE recovery, reject failed or downgraded STARTTLS upgrades—including a server that acknowledges STARTTLS without establishing TLS—and prove authentication cannot begin before TLS is established. Deterministic TLS-1.0-only and TLS-1.1-only servers must fail through both implicit TLS and STARTTLS during initial setup and IDLE recovery, while TLS-1.2-or-newer servers must succeed; a caller-requested TLS 1.3 floor must reject a TLS-1.2-only endpoint during both initial setup and IDLE recovery. Successful IDLE recovery must establish TLS, authenticate, and reselect the requested mailbox before delivering a callback. IMAP and SMTP transport modes must be independently configurable, include both mixed directions (implicit-TLS IMAP plus STARTTLS SMTP and STARTTLS IMAP plus implicit-TLS SMTP), and return the same complete capability and mailbox snapshot in every successful setup mode.
- Connection-lifecycle qualification must overlap both IMAP and SMTP setup for two mailbox connections with distinct credentials and distinguishable complete snapshots, then run overlapping sessions with distinguishable mailbox, body-part, UID mutation, SMTP, and Sent-append data and responses. Commands, results, body-part requests, two concurrent copies, two concurrent moves, concurrent SMTP submissions through the already-active sessions and their Sent appends, and IDLE callbacks must remain connection-scoped and appear exactly once on the owning transport; active-session SMTP overlap must rendezvous only the two submissions under test, independent of earlier setup submissions. Cancelling either connection's IDLE or in-flight operation must cancel its owning server command without cancelling or disconnecting either account, must preserve follow-up IMAP and SMTP work on each cancelled operation's owning session, and must not deliver delayed callbacks to either account, including after a recovered XOAUTH2 IDLE is cancelled; the peer body fetch must still be active with no cancellation event immediately before its explicit cancellation. Late IDLE callback suppression, including after recovery authentication is rejected, must be driven by a required deterministic fixture event rather than an elapsed-time assumption. Cancelling a transmitted COPY, MOVE, or Sent append must report an unknown operation outcome without replay and preserve both the owning session and a distinguishable peer. Pre-content SMTP cancellation must also qualify after the sender has been accepted, withholding message content, terminating the owning SMTP transport, and preserving session reuse; the fixture must signal transport termination before the gate inspects captured message events. Reusing SMTP after pre- or post-content cancellation, transport loss, or authentication rejection must terminate the invalid channel, reapply the caller's TLS floor, and reauthenticate before the next submission. Every secure-setup or authentication failure must close the failed service along with any service authenticated earlier in setup, and the fixture must signal that all setup work has quiesced before cleanup is asserted. An IDLE transport that disconnects must be closed before recovery, and the requested mailbox must be selected and IDLE entered before a recovered callback is delivered. Recovery authentication rejection must qualify for password and XOAUTH2, including XOAUTH2 challenge ordering, transport closure, and zero callback delivery. Follow-up IMAP metadata and SMTP usability probes must each complete within a bounded deadline. Closing a connected session, including after SMTP message content has been accepted, must itself complete within a bounded deadline, tear down only its two authenticated services, preserve other connected sessions and their follow-up work, terminate its in-flight reads, IDLE, UID mutations, SMTP submission, and Sent append without replaying state-changing commands, and reject every later operation without sending a command to the server; each post-close rejection must complete within a bounded deadline, and the fixture must signal deterministic closed-session quiescence before the gate compares captured commands and content. IDLE, body fetch, COPY, MOVE, and Sent append termination must each qualify independently without requiring same-session IMAP pipelining, including post-close rejection and quiescence; every state-changing close case must also preserve a distinguishable peer's IMAP and SMTP work.
- Deterministic incoming and outgoing MIME fixtures must cover supported transfer encodings, multipart alternatives, attachment metadata, and inline content.
- The engine must return verified source-to-destination UID mappings for move and copy. Mutation inputs must carry their connection-scoped message identities, and identities from another account must be rejected before either command reaches the server. Deterministic fixtures must capture the source mailbox, nonempty source UIDs and UIDVALIDITY, and destination mailbox actually sent for both commands, and successful fixtures must reorder the server-reported source sequence so destination identities cannot be zipped to request order. Empty, malformed, partial, mismatched-cardinality, repeated, nonpositive, or above-32-bit server UIDs and source or destination UIDVALIDITY values must be rejected through both candidate operations rather than only by a shared validator. Message-ID matching is not an identity-repair substitute.
- Move, archive, and trash require `MOVE` or targeted `UIDPLUS` removal plus a verified source-to-destination UID mapping such as `COPYUID`, including on servers that advertise `MOVE` without `UIDPLUS`. Both supported reduced-capability paths must map multiple source UIDs correctly when the server reports them in a different order. The deterministic `UIDPLUS` fallback must exercise the complete malformed-mapping matrix, including an empty `COPYUID` mapping, reject every invalid mapping before source removal, and prove the requested source UID is removed while an unrelated pre-deleted UID remains for a valid mapping. An unsupported move must not issue a move, destination-copy, source-removal, or expunge command, and the adapter must never invoke an unrestricted expunge fallback that could remove unrelated messages.
- UID/UIDVALIDITY reconciliation is the correctness baseline. Message identities must include their connection scope so two accounts with the same mailbox, UIDVALIDITY, and UID remain distinct and cross-account identities are rejected before any body request reaches the server. UIDVALIDITY must remain mailbox- and account-scoped, including while another account's same-named mailbox is actively resetting; every metadata entry must match the page UIDVALIDITY and use a valid UID, including invalid entries following a valid leading message; metadata pagination cursors and IDLE change/reset identities must stay within IMAP's positive 32-bit range, stale identities must be rejected before a body fetch, copy, or move command reaches the server, newly issued identities after a reset must work independently for body fetch, copy, and move, and selected body-part results must be bound to the requested mailbox, UIDVALIDITY, and UID. Mailbox snapshot comparisons are identity-based and order-independent while preserving duplicate multiplicity, so differently duplicated mailbox entries cannot compare equal. A reduced-capability MOVE fallback must never delete or expunge sources when its destination COPY is permanently or retryably rejected. `CONDSTORE` and `QRESYNC` are optional optimizations whose absence is acceptable only when the deterministic suite and both provider spikes meet the synchronization budget below.
- SwiftMail protocol trace and debug output may contain private data, so production logging must discard all authentication material, mailbox identifiers, and message content. Tests must exercise successful, challenge-response, and rejected authentication paths alongside body fetch, SMTP submission, and Sent append; capture every candidate-owned trace and debug path as raw bytes as well as the supplied product logger; decode standalone, field-prefixed, nested, and whitespace-wrapped base64 records before inspection; and prove passwords, bearer tokens, authentication exchanges, mailbox identifiers, and plaintext or encoded complete message payloads cannot reach any captured output.
- The engine must distinguish retryable and permanent IMAP rejections from transport failures whose command outcome is unknown, preserving the tagged response code needed for classification. Deterministic cases must prove successful and reduced-capability UID mutation commands are issued exactly once, permanent append, copy, and move rejections are not retried even with rewritten arguments, retryable copy and move failures preserve their response code and classification with a bounded command attempt, commands whose transport closes after the server receives them report an unknown outcome, and an uncertain copy, move, or Sent append is reconciled before any connection-scoped replay regardless of rewritten arguments.
- The engine must distinguish transient and permanent sender, recipient, and `DATA` rejections before message content, transport loss before the transaction and after sender acceptance but before `DATA`, explicit final `4xx` and `5xx` responses after message content, including the `499`/`500` class boundary, and a missing or otherwise indeterminate final response after message content. Every transport, authentication, sender, recipient, or `DATA` rejection before message content must prevent `DATA`, including when an earlier recipient was accepted. Only the indeterminate post-content case is an ambiguous delivery outcome that is not retried automatically; cancelling a submission after content must terminate its SMTP transport before reuse, and closing a session after message content but before the final response must return that ambiguity within a bounded deadline without replay while terminating both authenticated transports. An accepted response remains accepted when it has no parseable server message ID. The deterministic fixture must exercise real task cancellation before content transmission and must also capture and verify every recipient plus the exact raw message accepted by SMTP. An API that reports every error after entering its send call as ambiguous, or cannot distinguish an explicit rejection from an unknown outcome, does not qualify.
- Qualification must simulate an IMAP Sent append failure after confirmed SMTP acceptance and prove recovery retries only the sent-copy append without invoking SMTP send again, uses the discovered special-use Sent mailbox rather than a canonical name, appends the exact bytes accepted by SMTP, and returns a complete Sent mailbox identity whose APPENDUID UID and UIDVALIDITY are both valid positive 32-bit values. Concurrent Sent appends must vary and preserve both identity components.
- Pull requests run deterministic local IMAP/SMTP contract tests. Secrets-backed iCloud and Fastmail compatibility tests run only in an opt-in or scheduled environment, supplemented by a documented manual soak checklist.

## Synchronization budget

The deterministic synchronization fixture contains one mailbox with 10,000 messages whose metadata averages 2 KiB, plus separate no-change and 100-message-change runs after a completed backfill. The server applies a fixed 100 ms round-trip delay and 20 Mbit/s transfer limit. At the 95th percentile over 20 release-build runs on the iPhone 17 reference device, Initial Mailbox Availability must complete within 5 seconds, no-change and 100-message reconciliation within 10 seconds, and complete metadata backfill within 5 minutes. Incremental reconciliation may use at most 20 IMAP command round trips and download 5 MiB; complete backfill pages may contain at most 500 messages and use at most two fetch or search round trips per page plus ten setup and capability round trips. Synchronization may add at most 100 MiB of peak resident memory and must retain ADR-0018's 100 ms main-thread-stall cap. The iCloud Mail and Fastmail spikes use the same message counts and must meet the request, download, page-size, memory, and main-thread limits; their provider/network latency is recorded separately from the deterministic wall-clock thresholds.

SwiftMail will handle IMAP and SMTP setup verification as well as runtime operations if it qualifies, avoiding duplicate protocol stacks. The existing stream implementation remains only for POP3, which SwiftMail does not support. iCloud Mail and Fastmail are certified providers; manually configured servers receive compatibility-based support.

## Executable qualification boundary

`MailEngine.swift` defines the provider-neutral, transient engine façade before
any candidate dependency is adopted. It exposes negotiated transport and
capabilities, mailbox and stable UID/UIDVALIDITY results, bounded metadata and
selected body-part reads, IDLE, verified copy/move mappings, phase-aware SMTP
outcomes, and Sent append. It deliberately exposes no persistence, retry,
Mailbox Role mapping, or reconciliation API, keeping those decisions in
product-owned services.

`MailEngineQualificationTests.swift` supplies the reusable deterministic
contract. A candidate-specific test file in the `unwired-mailTests` target
provides a `MailEngineQualificationCandidateFactory` for the same fixtures,
including fixture-specific bound IMAP and SMTP endpoints, and must pass the
contract unchanged. The reference scripted candidate proves the
harness covers per-service TLS and authentication ordering, complete snapshots
across transport modes and accepted TLS 1.2 paths, including reduced-capability profiles with order-independent mailbox snapshots, certificate and server-identity failure teardown, overlapping connection setup plus connection isolation and cancellation across active SMTP sessions, UID mapping
and account-scoped UIDVALIDITY resets across every metadata page and both COPY and MOVE, rejection of invalid or inconsistent metadata identities and pagination cursors, rejection of invalid IDLE identities in mixed valid/invalid batches and invalid APPENDUID results, order-independent overlapping account metadata loads, order-independent mailbox and body-part results, exact connection-scoped paging, body-selection requests and returned body bytes, all-attempt malformed UID mapping rejection including mismatched MOVE source sets, COPYUID validation and exactly-once fallback before UIDPLUS source removal, targeted UIDPLUS source removal, bounded IDLE recovery waits with the TLS floor and server-identity validation reapplied,
successful password and XOAUTH2 IDLE recovery authentication before callback delivery, including XOAUTH2 challenge handling, owning-command cancellation and follow-up IMAP work, actual metadata mailbox/cursor/limit requests, pre-content SMTP task cancellation before the transaction and after sender acceptance plus post-content cancellation with exact owning envelopes and payloads, transient and permanent rejection after a recipient is accepted, accepted responses without message IDs, exact multi-recipient SMTP plus overlapping connection-scoped Sent-append payloads and identities,
Sent-append-only recovery, partial-setup cleanup, concurrent IDLE, body-fetch, same-type COPY and MOVE, and distinguishable SMTP outcome routing, pre-content SMTP termination on close without collateral transport teardown or shutdown callbacks, transmitted COPY, MOVE, and Sent-append cancellation and close-time termination without replay, reusable cancelled SMTP sessions, connection-scoped post-content cancellation, independently serialized IDLE and body-fetch close fixtures, challenge and rejection authentication-log coverage, unpadded and field-prefixed base64 privacy-trace inspection, and complete post-close candidate trace capture across distinct sessions.
Provider-backed, MIME, search, mutation, and performance qualification remain
the later gates already defined above.
